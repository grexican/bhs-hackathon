🌊 Wave or Particle? Yes.

Light and tiny things like electrons can act like a wave (spread out, rippling) OR like a particle (a single dot). Which one? Depends on how you look.

Think of it like: a Slinky. Held still it's just a coil of metal (a "thing"). Wiggle it and it becomes a traveling wave. Same object, two personalities.
Right now you're seeing the WAVE side — smooth ripples carrying energy.

🎯 The Double-Slit Experiment

Fire tiny particles at a wall with two slits. Logic says you'd get two stripes. Instead you get many stripes — an interference pattern — as if each particle went through both slits and rippled into itself.

Think of it like: two stones dropped in a pond. The ripples overlap, making big-and-flat spots. Quantum particles make that same pattern — even when you send them one at a time!

🪙 Superposition

Before you measure it, a quantum thing isn't "heads" or "tails" — it's genuinely both at once. The moment you look, it picks one. This is the heart of Schrödinger's famous cat.

Think of it like: a spinning coin. While it spins, asking "heads or tails?" makes no sense — it's a blur of both. It only becomes one answer when it lands.

🔍 The Uncertainty Principle

You can know where a particle is, OR how fast it's moving — but never both perfectly. Pin down one and the other goes fuzzy. It's a law of nature, not a bad measuring tool.

Think of it like: a photo of a race car. A sharp photo freezes its position but hides its speed. A blurry streak shows its speed but smears its position. You can't have both crisp at once.
Balanced: position is a little fuzzy and speed is a little fuzzy.

🪜 Energy Comes in Steps

An electron in an atom can't sit at just any energy. It can only live on specific "rungs," like a ladder. To jump up it must absorb an exact-sized chunk of energy (a quantum); dropping down spits out a flash of colored light.

Think of it like: stairs vs. a ramp. On a ramp you can stop anywhere. On stairs you're only ever on step 1, 2, or 3 — never on "step 2.4."
Electron is on energy level 1 (the ground floor).

👻 Quantum Tunneling

A tiny particle can sometimes pass straight through a wall it shouldn't have enough energy to climb. There's just a chance its "wave" leaks to the other side. Thinner walls = better odds. This is how the Sun shines!

Think of it like: rolling a ball at a hill it can't crest. In our world it always rolls back. In the quantum world, every so often it mysteriously appears on the far side.
Set a wall thickness, then send the particle and see if it tunnels through.

🧭 Spin

Particles carry a built-in property called spin. Measure it along an axis and you only ever get one of two answers: up or down — never anything in between. Tilt your detector and you change the odds.

Think of it like: a tiny magnet that, weirdly, always snaps to point fully with you or fully against you the instant you check — never sideways.
Detector pointing up. Odds of "up": 100%.

🔗 Entanglement "spooky"

Two particles can be linked so that measuring one instantly tells you about the other — even if they're on opposite sides of the galaxy. Einstein called it "spooky action at a distance."

Think of it like: a pair of magic gloves split into two boxes shipped to different planets. Open one and see a left glove — you instantly know the other is a right glove, faster than any message could travel.
