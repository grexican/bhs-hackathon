Lab 07 // through the wall

It crosses a wall it
could never climb.

Roll a marble at a hill too steep to crest and it always rolls back. A quantum particle doesn't always. Because its wave leaks a little way into the forbidden barrier, there is a real chance it simply appears on the other side without ever having enough energy to go over the top. Thin the barrier and the odds climb fast. This is how the Sun shines and how a USB stick remembers.

The probability drops off exponentially with width, so a barrier twice as thick is far more than twice as hard to cross. Tiny changes in width swing the odds enormously.

Why the Sun burns at all

Two protons in the Sun's core repel each other fiercely and, by classical physics, almost never have enough energy to touch and fuse. The Sun should be cold. Tunneling lets them slip through that electric wall often enough to keep fusion running for billions of years. You are warm right now because of a quantum loophole.

FUSION LOOPHOLE

The microscope that feels atoms

A scanning tunneling microscope holds a needle a few atoms above a surface and measures the tunneling current jumping the gap. Because that current is exponentially sensitive to distance, it can map individual atoms. The same exponential sensitivity makes flash memory work: electrons tunnel onto a gate to store a bit.

The wall is real. The particle just doesn't fully respect it.

Energy is still conserved; nothing here is free. The particle never has more energy than it started with, and it is never caught sitting inside the forbidden zone. It is simply that the wave description assigns a nonzero amplitude to "already on the far side," so sometimes that is where you find it. Solid walls are a classical approximation that quantum mechanics quietly ignores.
