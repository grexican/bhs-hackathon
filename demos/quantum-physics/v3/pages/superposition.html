Lab 02 // both at once

Not 0. Not 1. A live
blend of both.

A classical bit is a coin lying flat: heads or tails. A qubit is a coin spinning in mid-air, pointing anywhere on a sphere. The arrow below is a real quantum state. Steer it. The closer it tilts to the north pole, the more likely you get 0 when you measure. Hit measure and the spinning coin slams down to one face.

The percentages are not "we don't know yet". Before measurement the qubit is genuinely both, and the angle sets the odds. Measuring forces a choice and the arrow jumps to a pole forever.

The arrow is the answer

Every possible qubit state is one point on this sphere. North pole is a guaranteed 0, south pole a guaranteed 1, and the equator is a perfect fifty-fifty blend. Tilt the polar angle and you slide the odds. Quantum gates, the operations inside a quantum computer, are just rotations that move this arrow around.

PROBABILITY DIAL

Phase is invisible, until it isn't

Spin the phase angle and the measurement odds don't budge. So is phase useless? No. It is the secret ingredient that lets two qubits interfere, the same trick the double slit uses. Phase is hidden in plain sight on a single qubit and becomes the whole game once you combine them.

A qubit is not undecided. It is committed to being both.

The trap is to think the qubit secretly is a 0 or a 1 and we just lack the info. Decades of experiments rule that out. The blend is real and physical, and it is exactly why a handful of qubits can explore enormous numbers of possibilities at the same time. Measurement is the toll: the moment you read one out, the rich blend collapses to a single bit and the rest is gone.
