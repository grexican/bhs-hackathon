The Schrodinger's cat trap

Schrodinger's famous cat was not a prediction, it was a complaint. If a single atom can be in a superposition, and the atom's decay would trigger a poison, then the cat is formally both alive and dead until observed, which is absurd. He used it to show that taking the math literally leads somewhere strange. Where exactly the quantum fog becomes a definite cat is the heart of the puzzle.

SUPERPOSED → DECIDED

So what counts as "measuring"?

Does it need a conscious observer, or just any interaction with the wider world? Most physicists now favor decoherence: the moment a quantum system entangles with its messy environment, the delicate interference washes out and it behaves classically. No human required. But whether that fully explains the single definite outcome we see is still genuinely open, with several competing interpretations.

The math predicts the odds perfectly. The reason behind the single result is still argued.

This is the honest edge of the subject. The equations never miss: they tell you the exact probability of every outcome, verified to staggering precision. What they don't settle is why, out of all those weighted possibilities, you only ever experience one. Copenhagen, many-worlds, pilot-wave, and others all reproduce the same numbers while telling wildly different stories. Nobody has won the argument. That open question is a feature of quantum physics, not a flaw in your understanding.
