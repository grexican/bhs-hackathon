Lab 06 // spooky action

Two particles. One fate.
Any distance apart.

Make two particles together in the right way and their futures lock. Neither has a definite value on its own, yet the moment you measure one, the other instantly settles on the opposite, whether it sits on the next bench or across the galaxy. Einstein hated it and called it "spooky action at a distance." Decades of experiments say it is real. Fire pairs below and watch the tally.

In this perfectly anti-correlated pair the "same" bar should stay empty no matter how many trials you run. That stubborn perfection is the whole mystery.

Not a hidden note

The easy explanation is that the particles agreed on their answers before they split, like tearing a glove pair and mailing one of each. John Bell found a clever test: measure along tilted angles and count the matches. Pre-arranged answers can only go so far. Real entangled particles beat that limit. There was no note. The correlation is created at the instant of measurement.

BELL ANGLE TEST

No, you can't text with it

It looks like instant communication, but it isn't. Alice sees random outcomes; so does Bob. Only when they later compare notes, over an ordinary slower-than-light channel, does the eerie correlation show up. Relativity survives. What entanglement does power is quantum computing, quantum cryptography, and teleportation of quantum states.

The link is not a signal. It is a shared identity.

Before measurement the pair has no separate parts to speak of, just one joint state spread across both particles. Measuring is not Alice sending a command to Bob. It is the single shared state resolving all at once. Nothing travels between them because there were never quite two independent things to begin with.
